DeepSeek-R1 & V3 Prompt Engineering Standards

Architectural principles to extract maximum reasoning power from DeepSeek models.

Why does DeepSeek-R1 outperform with Zero-Shot?

Unlike older LLMs that required elaborate few-shot examples, DeepSeek-R1 was trained with large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) on long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) exploration. Providing strict few-shot examples actually biases and prematurely narrows its search tree. Give R1 the destination and constraints, and let its internal thinking loop find the proof.

What is the optimal Temperature for DeepSeek-R1?

The official DeepSeek team recommends setting the temperature between 0.5 and 0.7 (0.6 recommended). Setting temperature to 0.0 or 0.1 may cause recursive looping in the <think> phase, while anything above 0.8 causes mathematical hallucinations.

Why are XML Tags essential in DeepSeek Prompts?

DeepSeek's tokenizer and attention layers parse structured markdown and XML with high fidelity. Wrapping instructions inside <instructions>, data in <context>, and boundaries in <constraints> completely isolates directives from messy user data, eliminating prompt injection risks.

When should I choose V3 over R1?

Use DeepSeek-V3 for routine programming boilerplate, HTML/CSS generation, copyediting, and latency-sensitive API pipelines. Reserve DeepSeek-R1 for complex logic, competitive math, AST-level refactors, and multi-step algorithm proofs.
